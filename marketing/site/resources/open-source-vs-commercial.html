Open source vs commercial data erasure software

Both can wipe a drive. The question is what you need around the wipe — and what you're willing to give up to get it.

t tScrub Team · Last updated 22 Sep 2026 · 6 min read

The old trade-off was simple: commercial tools gave you a certified, supported product; open source gave you transparency and a price tag of zero — but you assembled the audit trail yourself. That line has blurred, which is exactly what makes the comparison worth doing properly.

The commercial case

  • Certification — third-party certification and auditor familiarity out of the box.
  • Support — a vendor to call when a drive misbehaves.
  • Completeness — an integrated product, from wipe to certificate.

The cost: licensing fees, potential lock-in, and a closed box you can't inspect.

The open-source case

  • Transparency — your security team can read every line of what runs.
  • Control — runs on your own hardware, no vendor appliance required.
  • Cost — no per-device licensing.

The historical cost: no certification, and the audit trail was your problem to build.

How tScrub splits the difference

tScrub is open and inspectable at the core — your team can audit exactly what tScrub does — with the compliance machinery already built in: signed reports, a certificate of destruction, and a verification path. You get the transparency of open source without having to assemble the evidence yourself. The free tier is genuinely free; paid tiers add vendor-signed, attributable reports and support.

FAQ

Is open-source erasure "less compliant" than commercial?

Compliance depends on the outcome and the evidence, not the licence. An open tool that produces signed, verifiable reports can satisfy the same requirements as a commercial one.

Do I need third-party certification?

Only if a client or regulator requires it. What you always need is erasure you can demonstrate — which both approaches can deliver.
