How to erase a Chromebook

A Chromebook's storage is soldered to the board and locked behind Google's bootloader, so you can't boot a wiper the way you would on a PC. You reset ChromeOS, then record that reset where the audit trail lives.

t tScrub Team · Last updated 21 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Chromebooks are laptops and tablets running ChromeOS, and almost all of them keep their storage as a soldered eMMC chip (newer Intel models use NVMe). There is no drive to pull and no BIOS that boots whatever you point it at — so erasing one is a two-step job: a factory reset on the device, and an attestation record in tScrub. Whether the appliance could ever boot the unit is decided by the processor architecture, and the two kinds are told apart the same way.

ARM or x86 — and why it matters

At the white “OS verification is off” screen, the bottom edge prints a hardware ID string. The prefix names the platform family:

Hardware IDPlatformCan tScrub boot it?
KIP… and other Intel IDsIntel x86Yes, via Developer Mode + Ctrl+L (SeaBIOS) — but the soldered eMMC is not yet discovered by the appliance, so the reset-and-attest workflow below is the supported path today.
BLAZE… (Tegra K1) and other ARM IDsARMNo — ARM Chromebooks have no legacy BIOS; Ctrl+U expects a ChromeOS-format image tScrub does not ship.

For either kind the supported workflow is the same: reset ChromeOS, then record the unit as an operator attestation. tScrub does not yet sanitise a Chromebook's eMMC directly on either architecture, so a Chromebook is processed as a device the appliance cannot wipe.

How to factory reset a Chromebook

A ChromeOS factory reset wipes local user data and restores the device to its out-of-box state. It does not need Developer Mode. Two ways, in order of preference:

  1. Powerwash, from ChromeOS. Sign in, then Settings → Advanced → Reset settings → Powerwash → Restart. If the device will not sign in, press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R on the sign-in screen instead.
  2. Recovery reset, when it will not boot. Hold Esc+Refresh and press Power to reach the recovery screen, then follow the prompts with a ChromeOS recovery USB (made with the Chromebook Recovery Utility on any computer). This reinstalls ChromeOS and wipes the device.

Enterprise-enrolled units first

A Powerwash does not un-manage an enterprise-enrolled Chromebook — after reset the device will force re-enrolment and lock again. If the unit is leaving your Google Workspace, deprovision it first: Google Admin console → Devices → Chrome → select the device → Deprovision. Skip the reset-and-attest flow until that is done, or the unit will not stay reset.

Recording the reset in tScrub

  1. Reset the device using one of the methods above, and note the serial (chassis label, or the bottom of the sign-in / recovery screen).
  2. Register the device. Devices → Add — enter the job's Chain of Custody ID, the serial, model and a note. This is a register entry only, so the device is on file and can carry the media record. A device that never booted tScrub has no row of its own until you add it.
  3. Record the storage as an attested item. On the Media page, add a record with media type eMMC/UFS (integrated), the device's make, model and serial, the reason Not supported by tScrub (cannot be booted), and the method Factory reset. The category is Laptop (or Tablet for a Chromebook tablet).
  4. Review the outcome. A Factory reset method records the device as retained — data wiped, hardware kept for reuse — never as destroyed. It appears on the certificate in the attested section, every line qualified operator-attested — not instrument-verified, and it is never counted as sanitised.

What the record does and does not claim

A factory reset destroys the user's encryption keys and local data, but it does not overwrite or purge the eMMC media. That is an honest difference, and tScrub records it as one: the method is Factory reset, the disposition is retained, and no NIST level, wipe result or SMART evidence is attached — there is no scan data behind the record. If a customer needs instrument-verified sanitisation of a Chromebook's storage, it is not available from tScrub today.

FAQ

Does a factory reset equal NIST Clear or Purge?

No. A factory reset destroys encryption keys and user data but does not sanitise the media. That is why tScrub records it as an operator attestation with disposition retained, never as a wipe.

Can tScrub boot an Intel Chromebook?

Technically yes — Developer Mode plus Ctrl+L boots the appliance through SeaBIOS. But the soldered eMMC is not yet discovered, so it cannot be wiped in place. Use the reset-and-attest flow.

What about ARM Chromebooks?

They have no legacy BIOS and cannot boot tScrub at all. The factory reset plus attestation is the only route, and it works on every model.
