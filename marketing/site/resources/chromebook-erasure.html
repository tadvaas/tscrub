How to erase a Chromebook

A Chromebook's storage is soldered to the board and locked behind Google's bootloader, so you can't boot a wiper the way you would on a PC. You reset ChromeOS, then record that reset where the audit trail lives.

t tScrub Team · Last updated 21 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Chromebooks are laptops and tablets running ChromeOS, and almost all of them keep their storage as a soldered eMMC chip (newer Intel models use NVMe). There is no drive to pull, and tScrub does not boot on them — so erasing one is a factory reset recorded in tScrub as an operator attestation. Four steps:

Step 1 — Put the computer in recovery mode

With the Chromebook powered off, press and hold Esc+Refresh and press Power. (Refresh is the circular-arrow key on the top row, where F3 sits on a normal keyboard; on a keyboardless Chromebook tablet, hold Power+Volume up+Volume down for ten seconds.) The recovery screen appears, with the hardware ID printed on the bottom of the screen — something like GNAWTY C5B-G2N-S2A-Q5V.

Step 2 — Identify the firmware, download and bake the USB

Take the first word of the hardware ID — that is the board codename, here GNAWTY. Go to cros.tech, type the codename into the search, open its device page, and click the Recovery button to download the image. An “end of life” banner only means the device no longer gets updates — the image still works.

Flash the image to an 8 GB or larger USB stick: unzip the download, then write the .bin to the whole USB device with dd on Linux/macOS, or Rufus in dd mode on Windows.

Step 3 — Insert the USB and wait for recovery to finish

Plug the recovery USB into the Chromebook, confirm on the recovery screen, and let it run. It reinstalls ChromeOS and wipes the device, then restarts into the out-of-box setup.

Step 4 — Check for MDM

The recovery screen cannot show enrolment, so check after the reset. When the unit restarts into the out-of-box setup, an enterprise-enrolled device demands an enterprise email, while an unmanaged one goes straight to sign-in. If it is enrolled, deprovision it in Google Admin (Devices → Chrome → select the device → Deprovision) — a reset alone never un-manages the device, and it will re-lock otherwise.

Recording the reset in tScrub

  1. Reset the device using the recovery reset in Step 3, and note the serial (chassis label, or the bottom of the recovery screen).
  2. Register the device. Devices → Add — enter the job's Chain of Custody ID, the serial, model and a note. This is a register entry only, so the device is on file and can carry the media record. A device that never booted tScrub has no row of its own until you add it.
  3. Record the storage as an attested item. On the Media page, add a record with media type eMMC/UFS (integrated), the device's make, model and serial, the reason Not supported by tScrub (cannot be booted), and the method Factory reset. The category is Laptop (or Tablet for a Chromebook tablet).
  4. Review the outcome. A Factory reset method records the device as retained — data wiped, hardware kept for reuse — never as destroyed. It appears on the certificate in the attested section, every line qualified operator-attested — not instrument-verified, and it is never counted as sanitised.

What the record does and does not claim

A factory reset destroys the user's encryption keys and local data, but it does not overwrite or purge the eMMC media. That is an honest difference, and tScrub records it as one: the method is Factory reset, the disposition is retained, and no NIST level, wipe result or SMART evidence is attached — there is no scan data behind the record. If a customer needs instrument-verified sanitisation of a Chromebook's storage, it is not available from tScrub today.

FAQ

Does a factory reset equal NIST Clear or Purge?

No. A factory reset destroys encryption keys and user data but does not sanitise the media. That is why tScrub records it as an operator attestation with disposition retained, never as a wipe.

Can tScrub erase a Chromebook directly?

Not yet — tScrub does not boot on Chromebooks today. The supported route is a factory reset recorded as an operator attestation, which is never presented as a media sanitisation.

What about ARM Chromebooks?

They have no legacy BIOS and cannot boot tScrub at all. The factory reset plus attestation is the only route, and it works on every model.

What if the unit has no resale value and is being scrapped?

Skip the recovery reset. Physical destruction is the sanitisation: shredding or crushing the unit destroys the soldered eMMC with it — a NIST 800-88 Destroy. Optionally run the recovery reset first to destroy the encryption keys as defence in depth. Record it in tScrub as an attested destruction — method Shredded or Crushed, reason Media soldered / integrated — not as a factory reset. It then appears on the certificate as destroyed, every line qualified operator-attested — not instrument-verified.
