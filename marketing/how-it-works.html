From boot to certificate in four steps

No installation. No vendor hardware. Just boot, wipe, and keep the proof.

01

Boot

Flash the image to a USB stick or PXE-boot it. tScrub runs on Linux with zero installation — the whole tool is one self-contained script you can inspect line by line.

02

Discover & classify

Every drive is detected across NVMe, SATA, and SCSI. Each one is mapped to its strongest supported capability — crypto/block/overwrite purge, enhanced erase, or nwipe clear.

03

Sanitise

All drives are wiped in parallel with live progress. Frozen, locked, or BIOS-blocked drives are flagged immediately rather than silently reported as clean.

04

Report

tScrub writes a chain-of-custody report — one row per drive with method, certification, and final status — tied to a single Chain of Custody ID.

What you need

  • • A Linux host with root (USB or PXE boot)
  • • nvme-cli for NVMe sanitise
  • • hdparm for ATA/SATA secure erase
  • • nwipe for SCSI/non-ATA drives
  • • sedutil-cli for SED/OPAL handling (embedded)

What happens to failed drives

If a drive is frozen, locked, or unsupported, tScrub records it with a PHYS_DESTR status instead of marking it clean. Route those drives to physical destruction and the report still shows the full chain of custody.
