# 22 — 2025–2026 BIOS reprogramming trends

**Status:** web + YouTube survey · 2026-10-23
**Scope:** what changed in tools, methods and password-removal software since the
original BIOS-unlock research, and what it means for the tScrub fleet.
**Related:** `21-hardware-bom.md` (fleet-weighted BOM), `10-hp-830g5-spi-sop.md`
(dominant model), `04-spi-flash-reflash.md` (workflow), `23-reddit-sweep.md`
(community threads and the Sure Start EC lead).

## 1. TL;DR — six changes that matter

1. **WSON8 in-circuit is the mainstream method.** The old "SOIC8 clip" advice only
   applies to SOP8. For WSON8 the community uses pogo-pin probes, gantry clamps, a
   solder-wire harness, or hot air + a WSON8 socket.
2. **The 830 G5 has two SPI chips.** U365 is 32 MB and U366 is 16 MB. Badcaps reports
   the BIOS password is in the **16 MB** dump and that **RCUnlocker** is applied to it.
   Dump both chips, not just the 32 MB one.
3. **Open-source password patchers have matured.** `dellpwn` (Dell, CVE-2026-40639),
   `8FC8_Patcher` (Dell 8FC8), `libv/hp_bios_passwd_erase` (HP 2009–2018) and the
   Badcaps **RCUnlocker** (HP) now cover a lot of what used to be a paid "send me your
   dump" service.
4. **Programmer market:** the XGecu **T48** remains the value standard. The **T56 is
   discontinued** and replaced by the **T76** (RISC-V, ~£177). The **RT809H** remains
   popular for EC/eMMC work.
5. **1.8 V WSON8 is increasingly common.** Dell Latitude 5530 (W25Q256JVEN) and
   ASRock B650M (25Q256JWEQ) examples are 1.8 V. A 3.3 V programmer can kill them.
6. **Toolchain:** `flashrom` is at **v1.7 (Feb 2026)** / **v1.8 (Aug 2026)**. NeoProgrammer,
   AsProgrammer and UEFITool remain the Windows-side standards.

## 2. Method trend — WSON8 in-circuit

| Method | How | Pros | Cons | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pogo-pin probe / gantry clamp** | Spring pins pressed onto the WSON8 pads | No soldering; fast | Pressure-dependent; can short adjacent pads; can damage nearby components | First attempt on a sacrificial unit |
| **Solder-wire harness** | Fine wires soldered to the pads | Reliable contact; no chip removal | High precision; risk of lifted pads | Pogo is unreliable, but you do not want to desolder |
| **Hot air + WSON8 socket** | Desolder, place in a WSON8-to-DIP8 socket | Most reliable; can verify the chip alone | Thermal risk; pad damage; slower | Pogo/harness fail, or the board is valuable |

**YouTube evidence (2024–2026):**

- **Salvamipc (Informatico)** — "Flash a WSON8 BIOS Chip Without Desoldering – Solder
  Wire Method" (17 Jun 2026, 5:11) and "WSON8 BIOS Flash Without Desoldering – No SOIC
  Clip Needed" (22 Jun 2026). The channel's whole premise is that SOIC8 clips do not fit
  WSON8.
- **Aditya11ttt** — "How To Flash A WSON Flat BIOS Chip (and why you should!)"
  (9 Nov 2024, 11:30).
- **Harshad Patel** — "ENIT POGO SET FOR WSON8" (42 s, 14k views) — shows the
  pogo-set approach for 6x5 and 8x6 WSON8.
- **Repair Channel** — "Removing the BIOS password from an HP Probook 440 G6"
  (6:40, 75k views, ~1 year old). Chapters show a CH341A + AsProgrammer workflow:
  detect, read, verify, edit the dump, erase, write, verify, reassemble.

**Our take:** pogo first, desolder if the ID or dumps are unstable. The pogo probe is a
consumable; buy two per size and inspect alignment under magnification.

## 3. The dual-chip HP finding (830 G5)

Badcaps thread "Help with HP 830 G5 bios" records:

- The 830 G5 has **two SPI flash ICs**: **U365 (256 Mbit = 32 MB)** and
  **U366 (128 Mbit = 16 MB)**.
- A moderator's instruction to a user whose 32 MB patch did not clear the password:
  **"use the RCUnlocker tool on the 16 MB bios"**.
- The same thread's earlier answer names the main chip as
  **`W25Q256FV@WSON8 8X6`**.

**What this means for us:**

1. Dump **both** chips on every 830 G5, with separate filenames and SHA-256s.
2. Try **RCUnlocker on the 16 MB dump first**. If the password is there, patch and
   reflash only that chip.
3. If RCUnlocker does not clear it, try the `libv/hp_bios_passwd_erase` family on the
   relevant dump, then UEFITool/NVRAM surgery on the 32 MB dump.
4. Do not assume the 32 MB main flash is the whole story. Our earlier dump analysis
   found password-related strings in the 32 MB image, so the two chips may both carry
   state; the empirical test is to patch one at a time and boot-test after each.

The same "two chips" pattern applies to the **820 G3** and **650 G4** (main BIOS + EC).

## 4. Software trend — open-source and community patchers

| Tool | Target | What it does | Status / confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`dellpwn`** (`R3n5k1/dellpwn`) | Dell, some HP | Rust TUI/CLI: scan a dump, recover Dell passwords, `clear-sivb`, `clear-password`, `clear-hp-password` | Released ~Jul 2026; CVE-2026-40639 / DSA-2026-197. Dell passwords are XOR-encrypted in the DVAR region, not one-way hashed, so recovery is deterministic from a dump |
| **`8FC8_Patcher`** (`craigsblackie/8FC8_Patcher`) | Dell 8FC8 suffix | Puts the BIOS into manufacturing mode, removes the password, keeps settings and BitLocker boot | Python; public since ~Mar 2026 |
| **`libv/hp_bios_passwd_erase`** | HP 2009–2018 | Clears `BiosUser`, `UserCred` or `NvramActiveRegn` sections | C; verified by the author on ProBook 650 G4 and EliteBook 840 G5 (2018); "newer laptops not supported" |
| **RCUnlocker** | HP | Badcaps community tool; reported to clear the 830 G5 password on the **16 MB** dump | Not open source; download from the Badcaps ecosystem; vet before use |
| **HPUnlocker** | HP | Older Badcaps HP unlock utility | Referenced in the 830 G5 thread; some users report it did not work, others say it does |
| **Lenovo BIOS Auto-Patcher** | Lenovo ThinkPad | Strips the supervisor password from a main-flash dump | Community tool (badcaps/vinafix), public mirror `suds-mcduff/lenovo-bios-autopatcher`; README says up to **8th gen**, so T14/X13 Gen 1 (10th gen) may be out of scope; EC may also need clearing |

**Caveat:** these are third-party tools that modify firmware. Run them offline, keep the
original dump and its SHA-256, and verify the patch is minimal before flashing. Do not
run untrusted "BIOS unlocker" binaries on customer dumps.

## 6. Voltage trend — 1.8 V is no longer rare

- **Dell Latitude 5530** uses `W25Q256JVEN` in a **WSON8 6x8** package; a Badcaps reply
  says the chip uses **1.8 V logic**. The thread documents a CH341A Black running at
  5 V, a 3.3 V mod, pogo-pin damage, and a T48 that would not auto-detect the chip.
- **ASRock B650M PRO RS** uses `25Q256JWEQ` (**1.8 V**) and can be blocked by status
  registers.
- **Newer HP** models may also be 1.8 V; we have not surveyed them yet.

**Rules:**

1. Read the chip marking: Winbond `...JV...` is 3 V, `...JW...` is 1.8 V.
2. Measure VCC at the chip before connecting the programmer.
3. Use a 1.8 V adapter; never assume 3.3 V.
4. Check the CH341A's VCC/logic level; some black clones run 5 V on the data lines.
5. If the chip ID reads `00 00 00` or the dump is unstable, stop; do not keep writing.

## 7. Toolchain versions (2026)

- **flashrom:** v1.6 (Jul 2025), **v1.7 (Feb 2026)**, **v1.8 (Aug 2026)**. Use v1.7 or
  newer. Linux-native, scriptable, hash-friendly.
- **NeoProgrammer / AsProgrammer:** Windows GUI for CH341A/T48/T76.
- **UEFITool:** inspect and patch UEFI volumes/NVRAM.
- **HxD / 010 Editor:** hex diff for the "buy one unlock, diff the patch" bootstrap.
- **WSL2 is still not recommended** for USB programmers; use native Windows or Linux.

## 10. What we should change in our plan

1. **Buy the T48, not the T56.** Add the T76 as an optional future purchase if
   Lenovo EC/NAND work grows.
2. **Add the 1.8 V adapter and a multimeter check** to every unit, not just newer HP.
3. **Dump both 830 G5 chips** (32 MB and 16 MB). Try **RCUnlocker on the 16 MB** first.
4. **Add a software stack:** `flashrom` 1.7/1.8, NeoProgrammer/AsProgrammer, UEFITool,
   RCUnlocker, `libv/hp_bios_passwd_erase`, `dellpwn`/`8FC8_Patcher` for any Dell volume.
5. **Add pogo-damage controls:** low pressure, board support, magnification, stop and
   desolder if pads look worn.
6. **T48 + pogo settings:** slow SPI clock to ~8 MHz; keep Check-ID and
   Verify-after-Read on; compare checksums across reads.
7. **Test on sacrificial units:** one 830 G5 (both chips) and one 650 G4
   (`libv` tool) before batch work.

## 12. Second-pass update (2026-10-23)

A deeper Google/Reddit/YouTube pass added four things this doc did not have:

1. **The Sure Start EC pin-short has an original source and a model list.** It is not
   just a Reddit rumour: Rhys Goodwin's blog (now 404, mirrored in
   `RhysGoodwin/rhysgoodwin.github.io/issues/4`) describes shorting pin 2 of the
   **16 MB Sure Start EC flash**, not the main BIOS. Confirmed on EliteDesk 800 G6
   Mini/SFF, 800 G4 Mini, ProBook 805 G6 and ProDesk 600 G6; fails on Elite Mini 600
   G9, EliteDesk 800 G9/G9M and ProDesk 400 G6 Mini. Risks: brick, HP Endpoint
   Security Controller Problem, 15 s splash timer.
2. **RCUnlocker is not guaranteed on the 830 G5, but the 16 MB EC chip is the target.**
   Parts listings describe the 16 MB `UC6` chip as the **"EC BIOS"**. Badcaps has
   independent 2023–2025 confirmations of RCUnlocker on that 16 MB chip
   (`25Q128JVSN`, refdes U366 or UC6); one user read it with a BeeProg+. A Bios-Fix 2021
   user reflashed both the `25Q256JV` and `25Q128JV` and the password remained. Treat it
   as BIOS-revision specific and test both chips.
3. **Lenovo T14/X13 Gen 1 SVP is in the MEC1663 EC.** The Auto-Patcher says it supports
   Lenovo up to **8th gen**, so our **10th-gen** T14/X13 may be out of scope; the EC
   routes are SVOD + JTAG pinout or a JTAG pin short (pins 4/8/10).
4. **WSON8 pogo best practice:** measure VCC on pin 8, use a proper pogo probe (cheap
   CH341A clips are "abysmal"), slow the clock to ~8 MHz, and desolder if unstable.

A third pass added GitHub to the sweep. The two useful open-source HP tools are
`libv/hp_bios_passwd_erase` and `R3n5k1/dellpwn`. Our 32 MB 830 G5 dump matches
neither: it has **0** `HpPassphraseStructureVariable` markers, 0 `$VSS`/`UserCred`,
and its 4 `NvramActiveRegn` blocks do not use the `H_FbPolicy`/`L_MpmPolcy` layout
`hp_erase` expects. That points the next test at the **16 MB EC BIOS**, not the main
dump.
